The Autonomous Business Reality Check

What happens when AI agents stop assisting businesses and start trying to run them?

AI-run businesses, examined as businesses

AI agents can build storefronts, write software, negotiate with customers and order stock. Whether those activities produce a business worth funding is a harder question.

This twelve-part series from LukiWebCo Research examines that gap. Its foundation is our AI Business Tracker, which ran from 2 to 27 October 2026: twelve hand-picked project case studies, four snapshots of NanoCorp’s public live directory, and a record of our own collection failures. The analysis also draws on operator accounts, payment-connected revenue disclosures, published experiments and enterprise research.

The evidence has limits. The case studies are not a representative sample. NanoCorp’s figures are platform-reported and unaudited. Verified gross receipts establish that customers paid; they do not establish profit. Missing figures remain missing.

Readers will find a practical framework for evaluating AI investments: distinguish activity from outcomes, account for human supervision, reconcile revenue with costs, detect stale information and decide when a pilot should stop. We apply the same scrutiny to our tracker, which continued publishing while individual collectors remained broken.
